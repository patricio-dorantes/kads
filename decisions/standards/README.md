# Standards evaluation of 2026-10-03

**Scope:** 31 candidate principles drawn from external documentation, provenance, and standards-design practice, evaluated for KADS 0.5.0. The result is KADS 0.6.0.  
**Namespace:** `kads-design`. Records `J-001` to `J-031` are investigation records under the tracked-record profile.  
**Authorship and review:** the candidates, records, and resulting changes were drafted by an automated agent. Six further automated agents challenged every candidate, and one more reviewed the integrated result. That is automated review, not verification by a person (clause 3.3.4). The repository maintainer is the author of record. The dispositions below are the drafting agent's proposals. The maintainer's act of tagging and publishing `v0.6.0` is their ratification; if you are reading this at that tag, the maintainer released them. What the maintainer personally examined is not recorded here.

These records explain why KADS says what it says and why it does not say more. They are not rules. Draft wording quoted inside them was not adopted unless the record says so.

## Method

The challengers' raw output, the first draft they reviewed, and the final gate findings are preserved unaltered in the [evidence bundle](evidence/2026-10-03-1/README.md).

The evaluation followed a brief supplied by the maintainer. Each candidate received an evaluation record with fixed fields and was challenged from six perspectives: minimalist, cognitive load, traceability, operations, AI and retrieval, and standards. The standards challenger also checked the factual claims made about each external standard. Each challenger was required to argue against the proposal or to state that no credible counterargument existed.

The layer value `GUIDANCE` means informative guides, outlines, registries' advisory text, or CONTRIBUTING. `CORE` means section 3 of the specification or its general clauses.

## What the challenge changed

The first draft proposed five new core clauses, a Procedure profile of five clauses, five new defined terms, and a two-axis table. Four challengers (minimalist, cognitive load, operations, standards) judged that package too large or too thinly evidenced for a release that also adopts a rule of restraint. The AI and retrieval challenger and the traceability challenger judged parts of it under-specified and asked for additions. The adopted result differs as follows.

- The Procedure profile was dropped. One hazard rule entered the core, because a warning in an optional layer does not reach an ordinary how-to page.
- The clause on epistemic roles and its five terms were dropped. One sentence in clause 3.2.2 does the work, and clause 3.3.2 now names assumption and hypothesis.
- The terminology clause and the tool-independence clause were dropped in favor of one sentence in clause 3.1.3 and advice in the adoption guide.
- The status clause survived but was rewritten by every challenger: one concept in place of four, a duty that arises when material is superseded, a note beside evidence that must not be altered, and visible text in the title because a banner protects only the first retrieved excerpt.
- Three rules were added that the draft did not contain, each proposed by a single challenger: verification claims say what was checked (3.3.5, traceability; observed case in the 0.3.0 changelog), uninspected sources should be identified (3.3.6, AI and retrieval; observed case in this evaluation's draft), and review by automated agents is named as such (3.3.4, AI and retrieval; anticipated, no observed failure). Their adopted wording was not seen by the six challengers. Only the final gate reviewer examined it, and on that review 3.3.6 was reduced from MUST to SHOULD and 3.3.5 and 3.3.4 were reworded.
- Citing a single requirement inside a clause was made possible now instead of being deferred.

Core placement went against the majority of challenger recommendations in four cases, and the records say so: J-010 (three for guidance, two for a profile, one for Core), J-029 (three for no change, one each for guidance, a profile, and Core), J-030 (three for no change, two for guidance, one for Core), and J-004 (four for a profile, two for Core, where the Core placement adopted is narrower than the profile).

Recorded dissents that did not prevail: a core rule for self-contained sections (AI and retrieval, J-014); the relation `refines` (traceability, J-018); a core clause for tool-independent sources (traceability, AI and retrieval, J-020); a basis requirement when a statement is promoted at its canonical home (traceability, J-015); one record with a table in place of 31 records (four challengers, J-019); a fuller Procedure profile (operations, J-004).

## Corrections found by the fact check

The first draft attributed several ideas to standards that do not state them. Each record carries the corrected source line. The errors were:

- According to secondary sources (no edition was inspected), the obsolete-document wording is in ISO 9001:2008 clause 4.2.3 g), a withdrawn edition; its absence from ISO 9001:2015 clause 7.5.3.2 is only partly verified.
- RFC 2119 section 6 reads "must be used with care and sparingly" in lower case.
- S1000D uses "first verification" and "second verification".
- Placing warnings before a step could not be confirmed as a requirement of ANSI Z535.6.
- W3C PROV says nothing about the standing of statements.
- Write the Docs defines docs as code as using the tools of code, not as format openness.
- DITA `keyref` is indirect addressing, not transclusion.
- "One topic per sentence" could not be verified for ASD-STE100 Issue 9.
- Clause 7.5.2 c) of ISO 9001:2015, which the draft cited, concerns review and approval and, according to secondary sources, states no independence requirement; other clauses and editions were not checked.

These errors are the observed case behind clause 3.3.6. The fact check also found an error already present in 0.5.0: the relationship registry mapped `supersedes` to a PROV relation of the wrong type. That is corrected.

## Limits

No adopter other than the one whose feedback is recorded under [`docs/reviews/`](../../docs/reviews/README.md) has used KADS. Most accepted changes rest on argument and on two observed cases from KADS's own history, not on adoption evidence. Paid or restricted standards, ASD-STE100, ATA iSpec 2200, ISO 9001, ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148, and the INCOSE guide, were not inspected; statements about them come from secondary sources or prior knowledge and are marked so in each record and in [sources](../../docs/sources.md). The challengers were automated agents sharing a model with the drafting agent, which limits the independence of the review.
