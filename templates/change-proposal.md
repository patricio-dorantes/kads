# Change proposal for KADS: \<what should change, in a few words\>

\<Use this outline to ask the editors of KADS to change, clarify, add, or remove a rule, guide, or outline. Save it as a new file for the `proposals/` directory of KADS, named `YYYY-MM-DD-short-title.md`, and send it as the proposals page of KADS describes. Keep the evidence in your own project. Remove these instructions and any section that does not apply. A proposal can be half a page.\>

- Date: \<YYYY-MM-DD\>
- From: \<a role and the kind of project; do not name the project, people, file paths, or record identifiers\>
- KADS version in use: \<version\>
- Concerns: \<clause numbers, guide, or outline; or "something missing"\>
- Kind: \<a rule that is wrong, unclear, missing, or too costly; or a problem in a guide or outline\>

## What happened

\<The case that prompted this, as an observation: what was written or done, what a reader then understood or did, and what went wrong. Say when, with which version, and how often it has happened. Keep what was observed apart from what you infer. Describe the case in general terms; do not include secrets, credentials, or details that identify people or systems.\>

## Evidence

\<What exists to support the account, and where it is kept. The evidence stays in your project; describe it here and say what the editors may see on request. Note anything redacted or withheld, and anything you do not know. If the case is anticipated and has not happened, say so plainly.\>

## What the rule did or did not do

\<Quote the requirement concerned, with its clause number and letter. Say whether it was followed when the problem occurred. A rule that was followed and still let the problem through is a different finding from a rule that was not followed.\>

## Proposed change

\<The smallest change that would have prevented the problem. Give wording if you have it. Say where it belongs: advice in a guide, an optional profile, or a rule for everyone. One more sentence in an existing clause is better than a new clause.\>

## Cost and alternatives

\<What projects that already follow KADS would have to do on the day they upgrade, and what the author of a short page would have to write. Alternatives you considered, and what would make you withdraw the proposal.\>

## What your project does meanwhile

\<The exception or local rule you recorded, with a link, so that the editors can see a working alternative.\>

## Disposition

\<Left empty by the proposer. The editors of KADS write here: accepted, accepted with changes, declined, or deferred; who decided; the reasons; what changed and where; what would reopen the question. After the decision the file leaves the directory; the changelog of KADS gives the commit where it can still be read.\>

## Proposer's response

\<Optional, added after the disposition. One of: agree; disagree but accept the decision; or disagree and ask for reconsideration, saying what evidence would decide it. A response stays with the proposal whatever the outcome.\>
